# `benefits_specimens/` — what is real in these documents and what is not

Three synthetic HMO benefit documents plus their ground truth, generated by
`data/build_benefits_specimens.py`. They exist so a benefits-schedule reader has
something to be tested against. **Nothing here is anyone's policy.**

| File | Type | Pages | The case it covers |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `individual_certificate_gold.pdf` | Certificate of Coverage | 2 | Prose sections, one sub-limit table. The easy case. |
| `corporate_summary_of_benefits.pdf` | Group Summary of Benefits | 3 | Dense grids, three member categories with different limits. The hard case. |
| `hr_one_pager.pdf` | HR advisory | 1 | Four facts and nothing else. The sparse case. |

Each PDF is also rendered to 150 dpi PNGs so the vision reader can be pointed at them
directly, since the reader takes images rather than PDFs. `groundtruth.json` holds the
expected extraction for all three.

## Why these are synthetic

Real benefit booklets cannot go in a repository. They are somebody's actual policy, they
name a member, and the copies circulating on document-sharing sites are re-uploads of
employer contracts that were never meant to be public. A generated specimen has none of
those problems and has one advantage a real document does not: we know the right answer
in advance, which is the whole point of a fixture.

## Real, and sourced

**Tier names, MBLs and room entitlements.** Taken from `hmo_published_tiers.csv`, which
was read off Maxicare's own plan page. Gold is ₱150,000 with a Regular Private room and
Silver is ₱100,000 with Semi-Private because that is what Maxicare publishes. See
[HMO_TIERS_PROVENANCE.md](HMO_TIERS_PROVENANCE.md), including its warning about search
results returning every tier shifted down one step.

**The shape of the documents.** Section order, the recurring "subject to MBL" phrasing, a
Special Modalities table of per-procedure caps, pre-existing coverage scaled by the number
of principal members enrolled, 80% reimbursement for emergencies at non-accredited
hospitals, benefits applied only after PhilHealth: all of that is how real published
schedules are laid out. The reference read was the Bureau of Customs' HMO procurement
documents of August 2025, Section VII, pages 25 to 33:
<https://customs.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/BIDDING-DOCUMENTS-HMO-FINAL.pdf>

Government agencies have to publish a full Schedule of Benefits so bidders can price
against it, which makes procurement documents the one reliable public source for this
material. The Office of the Solicitor General's 2024 Terms of Reference is the same
document class as a scan, useful if a scanned fixture is ever wanted.

## Invented, and not to be quoted as anyone's terms

Every peso figure other than an MBL. Specifically:

- all per-procedure sub-limits, including the cholecystectomy, MRI and lithotripsy caps
- the outpatient diagnostics ceilings (₱20,000 and ₱25,000)
- the first-year pre-existing caps (₱20,000 and ₱50,000)
- the 20% co-insurance on the Rank and File category
- the emergency reimbursement ceilings (₱30,000 and ₱40,000)

Also invented: the members, the employers, the certificate and policy numbers, the dates,
the hotline, and the 246-member headcount. The Bureau of Customs contract caps a
laparoscopic cholecystectomy at ₱15,000, and the specimens use ₱35,000 to ₱60,000
instead, deliberately, so that no figure in this folder can be mistaken for a quotation of
a real schedule.

Every page carries a banner and a diagonal watermark saying so. Do not remove them. An
unmarked specimen is the thing that gets mistaken for ground truth six weeks later.

## What these are for

`pricing/waterfall.py` currently models the MBL as the only ceiling on an HMO payment.
Real schedules stack at least four more constraints underneath it:

1. **A per-procedure sub-limit that binds before the MBL.** This is the big one. On a
   laparoscopic cholecystectomy the difference between "the MBL is the cap" and "the
   sub-limit is the cap" is roughly ₱90,000 on a ₱150,000 plan.
2. **Professional fees drawn from the same pot.** Our total excludes professional fees by
   scope, but the member's limit is consumed by them, so less of it is available for
   facility charges than we assume.
3. **An outpatient diagnostics ceiling**, which we model as a yes or no.
4. **Pre-existing coverage as a percentage of the MBL**, which we currently handle with a
   caveat rather than a number.

The `hr_one_pager.pdf` specimen exists to catch the opposite failure. Almost every field
is absent from it, and a reader that fills them in from what a Silver plan usually carries
is worse than one that returns null, because a confident wrong limit reaches the patient
as a peso figure.

## Regenerating

```bash
python data/build_benefits_specimens.py
```

Deterministic. Edit the builders in that file rather than the PDFs, and update
`groundtruth.json` through the script so the two cannot drift apart.
